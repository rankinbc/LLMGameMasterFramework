# Cistern Line - Playtest Summary (game 03)

## Game

**Title:** Cistern Line

**Premise:** You are the water engineer of Belt Authority tender Cistern, running outbound along a ten-stop asteroid mining line that can only be travelled one way. Each stop posts taskings from three factions (Miners' Union, Hauler Syndicate, Vesta Concession); each tasking costs water or fuel up front and resolves on a d10. Fuel burns on every jump and every docked turn, water can be cracked into fuel, and the mission is to dock at Terminus with two of the three factions at Standing +3 or better before the fuel or the 20-turn clock runs out.

**Rolls:** `SET=1 ROLE=33 LOOP=9 CLOCK=1 TWIST=14 WIN=8 MAP=1 TONE=6`

| Roll | Table result | What it became |
|---|---|---|
| SET 1 | Asteroid belt mining outposts | Ten outposts on the Belt mining line, each held by a faction |
| ROLE 33 | Water engineer | Water tender crew; water is the trade good and the fuel feedstock |
| LOOP 9 | Choose a contract, pay a cost, roll the outcome | TASK board per stop: cost up front, d10 vs target, closes on fail |
| CLOCK 1 | Fuel or food runs out | Fuel burns per jump and per docked turn; 0 = adrift |
| TWIST 14 | Each location can only be visited once | The line is one-way; skipping saves fuel but forfeits that stop's board forever |
| WIN 8 | Achieve a majority with three factions | 2 of 3 factions at +3 or better when docking at Terminus |
| MAP 1 | Linear track of 10 stops | Ceres Low to Terminus, jumps cost 2 fuel +1 per stop skipped |
| TONE 6 | Military procedural | Authority tender, TASK numbers, dockmasters, transfers logged on the wire |

Signature detail invented: the cracker (3 water to 3 fuel, no roll) plus stranded haulers that will accept either resource, making water and fuel one pool with a conversion tax.

**Rulesheet (verbatim):**

```
1. Cistern runs outbound along a 10-stop line. The line is one-way: a stop left behind can never be reached again.
2. Each turn: take one contract at your stop, crack water, or jump ahead. Jump costs 2 fuel, +1 per stop skipped. Every docked turn costs 1 fuel.
3. Contracts show a cost (paid up front, win or lose) and a target. Roll d10; meet the target to succeed. A failed contract closes and may cost more.
4. Success raises Standing with that faction; some contracts lower another's. Standing runs -3 to +5.
5. Factions: Miners' Union (U), Hauler Syndicate (H), Vesta Concession (C). At +2 or better, add 1 to your rolls for that faction. At -2 or worse, that faction's contracts close to you.
6. Crack water: 3 water becomes 3 fuel, no roll.
7. Fuel 0 anywhere but Terminus means adrift: game over.
8. Each jump draws one transit event on the way. Some cost fuel or water, some pay.
```

## Playthrough

The opening was ugly: two straight failures at Ceres Low (rolls of 3 and 2) burned a fuel and three water for nothing, and the player jumped out with an empty ledger. Pallas Drift turned it around with a 50 percent gamble on the melt plant (roll 7) for Union +2 and water back, then a squeaked audit (3+1 = 4) for Union +3, which unlocked a free 4-ton load waiting at Davida. At Hygiea the Dunlin water run landed Syndicate +2, but the scrubber refit failed on a natural 1 and dropped water to 4. The player skipped Vesta (Concession work would have cost Union points) and found a dry Syndicate hauler, Kestrel, hailing on the way to Thule; pumping her 2 water was a sure Syndicate +3, and a cheap Union certification at Thule pushed Union to the +5 cap with water down to 1. A second dry hauler, Tern, appeared on the Juno jump; with 1 water left the player paid her 2 fuel instead, which the GM allowed, leaving fuel at 8 for four jumps. A Rock Hound toll then took 2 fuel because there was no water to pay with, and the ship reached Davida with fuel 4 and three jumps ahead. The Union load plus one crack produced exactly 5 fuel, enough only by skipping Psyche; a lucky favorable-alignment draw gave 1 spare, and the player jumped straight to Terminus rather than risk a water-costing job that would have exposed the ship to a toll.

Result: WIN on turn 18 of 20. Final numbers: Fuel 1, Water 2, Union +5, Syndicate +4, Concession -1. Dice rolled on contracts: 7 (3 fails, 4 successes). Transit draws: 8.

## Rules fit

- Rulesheet: 8 lines, at the cap. Line 8 (transit events) was the one most tempting to cut, but it earned its place because the player used it to reason about the final jumps.
- Tracked numbers: Fuel, Water, Standing. Standing is really three numbers (U/H/C) presented as one list with rules attached, which the skill permits as one tracked thing, but it is borderline. In play it read fine because only two of the three ever mattered.
- One decision per turn: held throughout. Two things were handled outside the turn count: the player's opening question about which faction holds each stop (GM answered without spending the turn) and the `status` command (as specified).
- Status line: identical format every turn.
- Turn length: all turns under 180 words; the busiest (turn 10, Thule with five options plus the hail) was around 170 and had to be trimmed.
- Rules invented or clarified mid-game: (a) whether a hauler could be paid in fuel instead of water, ruled yes at parity since fuel is cracked water; (b) 5-B withdrawn once 5-A certified the same claim, which is logical but was not stated up front; (c) the Rock Hound toll takes fuel when water is short, stated in the event text at the moment it happened; (d) arriving at Terminus on exactly 0 fuel is a dock, implied by rule 7 and never tested. None felt unfair; (a) was a genuine ruling the player could have been refused.
- Nothing had to be memorized beyond the rulesheet. The +3 Union sponsor bonus and the Rock Hound toll both arrived as one-line reveals through play.

## Decisions

18 turns played plus one free question and one `status`.

- Genuinely hard (7): T1 (which faction to open on), T3 (take Marigold with the U-1 or leave Ceres empty), T4 (50 percent melt plant vs 70 percent audit), T8 (scrubbers for fuel vs move on with water at 6), T9 (skip Vesta entirely), T10 (Kestrel vs stockpile sale vs Union claim with 4 water), T13 (pay Tern in scarce fuel vs eat H-1).
- Medium (3): T2 (second task after a fail), T7 (Dunlin vs scrubbers, both good), T11 (buffer U or save the water).
- Obvious (8): T5 (80 percent audit), T6 (jump), T12 (jump), T14 (jump to Davida), T15 (take free load), T16 (crack), T17 (skip Psyche, forced by arithmetic), T18 (jump home).

The hard choices clustered in the first 13 turns. From T14 on the game became a fuel ledger with one correct line, tense but not a choice.

## Emergence

Moments where the systems produced something unplanned:

1. Two opening fails on 3 and 2 left Ceres Low with only the Marigold job, which the player refused on faction grounds, so the first stop produced nothing at all. The game stayed viable because the Union stop next door had a +2 job.
2. Union +3 at Pallas unlocked the Davida free load, which five turns later became the only fuel source left on the line.
3. The Kestrel hail interacted with the Thule stockpile sale: both wanted the same 4 water, and the sale's H+1 would have been cancelled by abandoning Kestrel. The deck turned a simple sale into a net-zero trap.
4. The second hail (Tern) landed when the player had 1 water, forcing the free-text fuel-for-water offer and a ruling.
5. The Rock Hound toll took 2 fuel precisely because the player had already given his water away, pushing fuel from 6 to 4 and making Psyche unreachable.
6. The skip-jump pricing (2 +1 per stop skipped) meant Davida to Interamnia direct cost 5 instead of 6, which was the only line that reached Terminus. The player found it himself at T15.
7. Two favorable-alignment draws (+1 fuel each) on jumps 1 and 2 were pocketed early; two more on jumps 7 and 8 were the actual margin at the end. Without them the final jump would have been on exactly 0.

Count: 7.

## Problems

- The Concession was a dump faction. Every Concession job cost Union standing, and with "2 of 3" as the win, the obvious plan was to ignore one faction completely. Standing C ended at -1 and never mattered. A three-faction majority needs some reason to touch all three (a Terminus docking fee payable in Concession standing, or Concession-only fuel).
- The endgame (T14 to T18) was arithmetic, not decisions. Once two factions were at +3 the only question was fuel, and the fuel question had one answer. A late Standing threat (a faction event that could drop a +3 to +2) would keep the second half honest.
- Standing +5 cap with nothing to spend it on. The player banked U+5 as a buffer against a loss that never came.
- The transit deck repeated (hail on draws 3 and 4). Not broken, but a second identical hail felt like the deck was small. It is.
- Early fails at Ceres Low (two low rolls on 60 and 70 percent jobs) are the kind of swing that could sour an opening; the recovery was available but a weaker position could have been a loss by turn 10 through no decision of the player's.
- The GM answered the opening line-manifest question for free. Defensible, but the skill says everything other than the four commands is a move; a strict reading would have spent the turn.
- Busy turns pushed the 180-word cap; five options plus a hail plus arrival prose is the limit of what fits.

## Fun rating

**7 / 10.** The water-fuel conversion and the skip-jump pricing gave real arithmetic to do every turn, and the hauler hails plus the toll made transit feel like a system rather than a loading screen. It loses points because one faction was ignorable by design and the last five turns were a solved line with the dice deciding the margin.

## Would replay

Yes. The contract boards, hail timing and toll draws would reseed, and I want to try the Syndicate-plus-Concession route with a fuel-positive economy to see whether the Union can be ignored the same way, which would confirm or refute the dump-faction problem.
