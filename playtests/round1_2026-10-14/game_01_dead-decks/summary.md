# Dead Decks - playtest summary

Playtest game 01, 2026-10-14.

## Game

**Title:** Dead Decks

**Premise:** You command a twelve-trooper platoon aboard the derelict generation ship Meridian Vow. Quartermaster Dessa Vosk mutinied, spaced your original command, and now holds the only working lifeboat in the Aft hull. The ship's three surviving habitat hulls (Fore farms, Mid armory, Aft engineering) are separated by depressurized dead decks that Vosk's patrols haunt. You run cargo between the hulls to pay your troopers' upkeep, build enough chits to buy the lifeboat's Core off the bay crew, and then storm the bay and launch.

**Rolls and what they became:**

| Roll | Table result | In the game |
|------|--------------|-------------|
| SET=4 | Derelict generation ship | The Meridian Vow, three live hulls joined by dead decks |
| ROLE=28 | Hero with a grudge and a small army | Platoon commander hunting the mutineer who spaced your old unit; Troopers is a tracked number |
| LOOP=1 | Buy low, sell high across moving prices | Four goods, three hull markets, prices shown only on site, drifting every turn |
| CLOCK=18 | Each turn costs a fixed upkeep | 3 chits per trooper per turn; shortfall causes desertion |
| TWIST=7 | Night turns double all risk and all reward | Dark cycle every third turn: ambush range, loot, scavenge kills and yields, and price drift all double |
| WIN=2 | Reach a location and get out | Reach Aft, buy the Core, win the bay assault roll, launch |
| MAP=7 | Three islands and the sea between | Fore, Mid, Aft hulls with the dead decks as the sea; any-to-any crossings |
| TONE=6 | Military procedural | Upkeep ledger, sergeant's reports, roll-and-consequence voice |

Signature detail: the named rival Dessa Vosk, whose Core price rises 20 every Dark cycle.

**Rulesheet (verbatim):**

```
1. Three hulls: Fore (farms), Mid (armory), Aft (engineering, Vosk's lifeboat bay). Crossing between any two takes a turn through the dead decks.
2. One action per turn: Trade (sell any cargo here, then buy one good), Cross, Scavenge, Recruit (Mid only, 1 trooper for 30 chits), or Launch (Aft only).
3. Each trooper carries 1 crate and eats 3 chits at end of turn. Short on chits: 1 trooper deserts per 3 chits short.
4. Goods: Ammo, Air, Seed, Med. Each hull has its own prices, shown only while you are there; they drift every turn.
5. Cross: roll d6, ambush on 1-2. Fight: d6 + troopers/4 (round down), need 5+. Win: loot d6 x 10 chits. Lose: d3 troopers die with their crates.
6. Scavenge: roll d6. On 1 a trooper dies; otherwise gain that many crates of a random good (needs free hands).
7. Dark cycle on turns 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18: ambush on 1-4, scavenge kills on 1-2, and loot, scavenge yields, and price drifts all double.
8. Launch (Aft): buy the Core at the listed price (rises 20 each Dark cycle), then roll d6 + troopers/3 (round down), need 7+. Fail: lose d3 troopers, keep the Core.

Goal: Launch the lifeboat from Aft.   Lose if: troopers hit 0 or turn 20 ends.   Turns: 20
```

## Playthrough

The player opened by spending nearly half the bankroll on 12 Seed at the farm hull, then crossed to Aft and was ambushed on a roll of 1, winning the fight easily and pocketing 20 chits of loot. At Aft, in the first Dark cycle, the seed sold for 300 and the player bought 12 Air at 8, which left the platoon 40 chits short of the Core while standing in the bay's shadow. Rather than sell the Air back and gamble on a two-in-three launch with no reserve, the player ran the full triangle: Aft to Mid (second ambush, fight won on an exact 5, 60 loot), sold Air at 34 and bought Ammo at 12, tried to recruit three at once and was held to one by the rules, then crossed to Fore (third ambush in three crossings, won again with 60 loot). Ammo sold at 30, 13 Seed bought at 7, and the player asked for status before deliberately crossing back to Aft in the dark, having worked out that with +3 the fight only fails on a 1. The dark crossing produced a fourth ambush, won on another exact 5 with a doubled but tiny loot of 20. Aft Seed had spiked to 33 on the dark drift, so the seed sold for 429 and the player bought 13 Air as a cheap hedge against a failed launch. An event then delivered two deserters from Vosk's bay crew; the player hired both, reached 15 troopers for a +5 bay bonus, bought the Core for 300, and rolled 5 + 5 = 10 to launch. Result: win on turn 11 of 20 with 542 chits, 15 troopers, and 13 Air left behind.

## Rules fit

- Rulesheet: 8 lines. Within cap, though lines 1, 2, 5, 7 and 8 are long compound sentences doing the work of two lines each.
- Tracked numbers: Chits, Troopers, Cargo (list with capacity and upkeep rules). Three. The status line also carries Core price and Light/Dark, which are world facts rather than player-owned numbers, but the player does arithmetic on Core every turn, so in practice it is a fourth number.
- One decision per turn: held. The deserter offer on turn 11 was handled as a free rider attached to the move, which bends the rule slightly.
- Status line: same format every turn.
- Turn length: every turn under 180 words; most were 90 to 130.
- Rules that needed clarifying or inventing mid-game:
  - Recruit is one per turn (the player asked for three). The rulesheet says "1 trooper for 30 chits" and the GM read that as a cap; it should say "one per turn" outright.
  - "Hold" was not in the rulesheet and was offered as a numbered option on turn 9. Upkeep still applies; that was stated when offered.
  - Whether hiring event deserters consumes the turn's action. GM ruled it does not.
  - Scavenge "needs free hands" made the action dead for the entire game because trade kept every hand full.
  - Cargo at launch is simply lost; not stated anywhere.
  - Whether an event-induced Dark turn also raises the Core price. Never came up because the game ended before turn 14.

## Decisions

- Hard (genuine tradeoff with a real downside either way): 3. Turn 4 (sell the Air and launch at 67 percent with no reserve, or run the loop), turn 9 (cross in the dark or pay a turn of upkeep to wait), turn 10 (buy the Air hedge or go in unencumbered).
- Medium (some thought, but one option clearly better once the prices were known): 2. Turn 2 (which hull to cross to with unknown prices), turn 6 (recruit or cross in the dark).
- Obvious: 6. Turns 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 11. Once the Seed, Air, Ammo triangle was discovered on turns 3 and 5, every trade turn was a solved problem.

11 turns played; 3 of 11 had a hard choice.

## Emergence

1. Four crossings, four ambushes (rolls 1, 1, 2, 3), all four won, two of them on an exact 5. The "danger" of the dead decks turned into 160 chits of loot and became a running joke the GM narrated honestly.
2. Turn 4: arriving at the Aft bay with 220 chits against a Core price of 260, with 96 chits of Air in hand. The numbers created a real temptation the design never scripted.
3. The dark-cycle drift doubled and happened to hit Aft Seed for +8 while the player was carrying 13 Seed toward Aft, turning a planned 25-per-crate sale into 33.
4. The player's turn-9 realization that at 13 troopers the fight check only fails on a 1, so a dark crossing is positive expected value. The twist meant to add risk added reward only.
5. The deserter event landing on the turn before launch, lifting the bay roll from needing 3+ to needing 2+. Fair, random, and well timed.
6. Mid Seed quietly collapsed from 18 to 8 over ten turns of drift without the player ever seeing it; a missed opportunity that would only show on a replay.

Count: 6 (5 visible to the player).

## Problems

- Too easy. Won on turn 11 of 20 with 542 chits unspent, never below 52 chits, never lost a trooper. The trade triangle nets roughly 100 chits per turn against upkeep of 36 to 39 and a Core that rises about 7 per turn. The clock never bit.
- The fight check saturates. With 12 troopers the bonus is +3 and only a roll of 1 loses; ambushes are worth an average 35 chits each (70 in the dark). Crossing is a profit center, which inverts the intended risk. The bonus should be troopers/6, or ambush losses should scale with cargo value.
- The 20-turn budget was never close to binding. Either the Core should start near 400 and rise 40 per cycle, or upkeep should be 4 to 5 per trooper.
- Scavenge was never used and never attractive. With trade filling every hand, there is no reason to roll for random crates.
- Recruit at one per turn was too slow to matter; the player only recruited once and got the rest from a random event. The troopers/3 breakpoints (12, 15, 18) are a good idea that the recruit rate undercuts.
- Med was never traded. Its spreads were real (Mid 14 to Aft 32) but the Seed, Air, Ammo triangle covered every leg, so a fourth good was dead weight.
- The Dark twist barely registered: one doubled loot of 20, one doubled drift. It should also double buy prices or make Vosk's patrols stronger, so the player has a reason to avoid dark crossings.
- Vosk never acted. A named rival who only raises a price is a line in a ledger, not an opponent. A patrol that hunts the richest hull, or a garrison that grows, would make the grudge mean something.
- Repetition: turns 5 through 10 were the same cross-sell-buy beat three times. Fine for a trading game, but with nothing pushing back it read as a loop rather than a build.

## Fun rating

6 out of 10. The first four turns were excellent: visible arithmetic, a tight bankroll, an ambush on the first crossing, and standing 40 short of the Core in the bay's shadow was a genuine decision. After turn 5 the economy outran every pressure in the game, and the last six turns were execution of a plan that could not fail.

## Would replay

Yes, once, with the clock tightened. The hull triangle, the upkeep-versus-capacity trade on trooper count, and the /3 and /4 breakpoints are a solid chassis worth optimizing again, but as written the route is solved after one pass and the rival never shows up. Double the Core price and make ambushes scale with cargo and it is an 8.
