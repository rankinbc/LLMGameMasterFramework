# Cold Burn - playtest summary (game 04)

## Game

**Title:** Cold Burn

**Premise:** Two years of static orbital war over Thessaly-4 have left eight fortified sectors ringing the dying ground base Hollow. You are the admiral of four ships (Castellan cruiser, Vesper destroyer, Mule freighter, Pike scout corvette) with one sealed deuterium core aboard the Mule. The ring is a trench line in orbit: nothing moves because anything that moves gets shot, and your low orbit burns reaction mass every turn just to stay up. Get the Mule to sector 5, directly over Hollow, and bring it down through three turns of descent before the tank runs dry.

**Rolls:** SET=27 ROLE=5 LOOP=13 CLOCK=18 TWIST=11 WIN=7 MAP=2 TONE=4

| Roll | Table result | What it became |
|---|---|---|
| SET 27 | Trench front, static war | A ring of fortified orbital sectors with dug-in railgun emplacements that never move |
| ROLE 5 | Fleet admiral with 4 ships | Castellan 10 hull (4+ gun), Vesper 6 (5+, cheap mover), Mule 4 (cargo), Pike 3 (6+, scout sight) |
| LOOP 13 | Ration a shrinking supply across needs | One shared RM tank spent on moves, evasion, descent, with scuttling as the pressure valve |
| CLOCK 18 | Each turn costs a fixed upkeep | 1 RM per surviving ship per turn; signature detail: doubles at perigee on turn 9 |
| TWIST 11 | Damage is permanent; nothing repairs | Hull never recovers; killed guns stay dead; a scuttled ship is gone |
| WIN 7 | Deliver a cargo intact | Mule (hull 4) must land; Mule dead means core dead |
| MAP 2 | Hex ring of 8 around a center | Ring sectors 1 to 8, Hollow at the center under sector 5 |
| TONE 4 | Hard sci-fi | Railguns, delta-v as the only currency, no shields, tethered crew transfers |

Signature detail: the perigee pass on turn 9, after which upkeep doubles. Hidden setup: sectors 2 to 8 shuffled from gun, gun, gun, mines, debris, cache, clear; the roll gave 2 debris, 3 clear, 4 mines, 5 gun, 6 gun, 7 gun, 8 cache (tanker Yoke, +12 RM).

**Rulesheet (verbatim):**

1. Four ships on a ring of 8 orbital sectors around Hollow, the base at the center. You start in sector 1. A sector's contents are unknown until a ship enters it; Pike also sees both neighboring sectors.
2. One shared tank of reaction mass (RM). Upkeep of 1 RM per surviving ship is paid at the start of every turn. RM 0 means the fleet deorbits and you lose. Scuttle a ship any time to end its upkeep.
3. Each ship does one thing per turn. Move one sector costs 2 RM (Vesper 1). Evade costs 1 RM and makes guns need 6+ on that ship this turn. Hold costs nothing.
4. Each enemy gun fires once per turn at the largest ship in its own or a neighboring sector (Castellan > Vesper > Mule > Pike): roll 4+ hits for 2 hull. A gun that fires is revealed.
5. A ship that holds may shoot one revealed gun in its own or a neighboring sector: Castellan hits 4+, Vesper 5+, Pike 6+. A hit kills that gun.
6. Descent: from sector 5 only, 6 RM, takes 3 turns. During descent Mule cannot evade or abort, and guns in sectors 4, 5, 6 fire at Mule first, each turn.
7. Nothing repairs, on either side. A ship at 0 hull is gone. Mule carries the core; Mule gone, core gone.
8. Win: Mule lands (third descent turn done). Lose: Mule destroyed, RM 0, or turn 16 ends.

## Playthrough

The player opened by splitting cheap probes both ways: Pike found a debris field at 2 and read 3 as clear, while Vesper stumbled onto the derelict tanker Yoke at 8 for +12 RM and immediately took (and dodged) a shot from a gun in 7. Reading a 5+ vs 4+ duel as a losing trade, the player pulled Vesper back and committed clockwise, using Pike's two-sector sight to spot the minefield in 4 before anything heavy touched it. Pike then went into the mines alone to look at 5, rolled clean, found a gun, and survived its one shot. From there the pattern was set: Castellan went in as the designated target, took a hit each time a new gun opened up (10 to 8, then 8 to 6), and killed each gun with its 4+ rail on the first shot, first in 5, then in 6. Mule was walked up behind the escorts and never had a gun in range of it; its one real risk, the mine roll in 4, came up a 1. With all three descent-covering sectors cleared by turn 7, the game became pure arithmetic: a status check at turn 8 showed that keeping four ships through the doubled perigee upkeep would land with 1 RM, so the player scuttled Pike and kept Vesper as insurance. Descent began turn 9 and the Mule touched down on turn 11 with no gun ever firing at it.

Result: WIN, turn 11 of 16. Final numbers: RM 7, Castellan 6, Vesper 6, Mule 4, Pike scuttled, guns 5 and 6 killed, gun 7 untouched. Dice used: 10 rolls (6 enemy or mine rolls against the player, of which 2 hit; 4 player rolls, of which 2 hit).

## Rules fit

Within caps: 8 rule lines exactly, 2 tracked numbers (RM, hull list; ship positions are map state, not a tracked number), one decision per turn, 3 to 5 options every turn, status line in the same format every turn, every turn under 180 words, dice shown with target numbers.

Things that had to be clarified mid-game and were surfaced through option text rather than a rules change:
- Evade vs. shoot exclusivity. Rule 3 says each ship does one thing; rule 5 says a ship that holds may shoot. Whether evading counts as holding was not spelled out. The GM treated evade as the ship's action (no shot) and said so in a turn 4 option.
- Move then descend in one turn. Covered by "each ship does one thing per turn" but the player had to notice the Mule needed a separate turn to enter 5 before descending. Not unclear, just an unstated consequence.
- Mines on the Mule: a mine hit is 2 hull, same as a gun hit; no ambiguity, but Mule at 4 hull survives exactly one mine and one gun hit, which is the number the whole game turns on and the rulesheet never says it out loud.

The rulesheet is dense for 8 lines; a first-time reader has to absorb targeting order, gun ranges, and the descent window before turn 1. It fit, but barely.

## Decisions

Turns played: 11 decision turns plus one status call.

- Genuinely hard: 5 (T1 probe split vs. group move; T3 sending Pike alone into mines to look at 5; T4 who absorbs gun 5's fire; T7 stage the Mule into the mines now vs. wait for gun 6 to die; T8 scuttle math under the perigee doubling).
- Obvious: 6 (T2 pull Vesper back; T5 shoot the revealed gun; T6 repeat the Castellan-plus-Pike drill; T9, T10, T11 descent turns with nothing to do).

The three descent turns are the weak spot: once every gun in 4, 5, 6 is dead, the descent is a formality and the only live question is whether to scuttle for RM, which the player had already settled.

## Emergence

1. Turn 1: a 1 RM probe with Vesper paid off with +12 RM from the derelict tanker and at the same time revealed gun 7 by drawing its fire; the player had not planned to learn anything about the far route that early.
2. Turns 2 and 3: Pike's two-sector sight turned the minefield at 4 from a trap into a known cost, which shaped every later move (who goes in first, when the Mule follows).
3. Turns 4 and 6: the targeting-order rule (largest ship in reach) made Castellan a deliberate sponge; each new gun announced itself by putting a 2-hull gouge in it, and with nothing repairing, Castellan went from 10 to 6 as the price of information.
4. Turn 8: the perigee doubling turned a comfortable 33 RM into a landing margin of 1 RM with four ships or 7 with three, forcing the scuttle decision. The clock, not the guns, produced the game's tightest moment.

Count: 4. Nothing scripted; all four came from fog, targeting, and upkeep interacting.

## Problems

- Too easy on this seed. The random layout put the mines and only one gun on the short clockwise route and stacked three guns on the long route the player never took. Add kind dice (4 of 4 mine rolls clean, 2 gun kills in 2 Castellan shots) and the Mule was never shot at. A mirrored layout would have been a very different, probably much harder game.
- Ended at turn 11, under the skill's 12-turn floor. Minimum possible is 7 (4 Mule moves plus 3 descent turns), so the 16-turn budget is mostly slack that only bites through the perigee upkeep.
- The targeting-order rule is strong and fully predictable: as long as Castellan stands within reach of the same gun as the Mule, the Mule is untouchable. The escort game was solved from turn 4 on. Guns that sometimes target the Mule (or the smallest ship) would restore tension.
- The last three turns had no real decision. Descent could be two turns, or the escorts could have a job during it (a gun that walks into range, a debris shower, a second upkeep spike).
- Pike's 6+ gun is close to decoration; it took one pointless shot. Vesper did nothing after turn 1 except cost upkeep and give the player a feeling of insurance.
- "Deliver a cargo intact" lost its "intact" dimension: Mule is either alive or dead. There is no partial outcome (a damaged core that powers Hollow for a month instead of a year).
- RM 80 start felt loose for the first eight turns; the pressure only arrived with the doubling. Starting at 64, or doubling at turn 7, would make rationing matter from the opening.

Nothing was broken or unfair. Repetitive: the Castellan-forward, Pike-looks, Castellan-shoots loop ran three times in a row.

## Fun rating

7 / 10. The opening and midgame were the good kind of arithmetic: every move had an RM cost I could compute against a known upkeep, and finding the tanker with a throwaway probe felt like a real trading-game score. It lost points because once I understood that Castellan soaks everything and the rails hit on 4+, the win was never in doubt, and the final three turns were just watching the counter tick.

## Would replay

Yes. The hidden layout is the whole puzzle, and a seed that puts two or three guns on the short route, or mines directly under 5, would force the far-side route past gun 7 and a real fight for the descent window. I would want the targeting rule loosened and the descent shortened first, but the ration-versus-risk core is sound and replaying with a fresh ring is exactly the kind of mastery loop I like.
