# Summary: Ice Gauge (game 09)

## Game

**Title:** Ice Gauge

**Premise:** A fever has shut the river town of Harrow's Crossing. You run the quarantine command's drone shed on Ferry Island with one Sparrow quadcopter and one broken Cormorant strike drone. The barge Marigold is running fever-carriers past the cordon every night on a circuit between the town's three islands. Rebuild the Cormorant part by part, paid for in two currencies that trade at a drifting rate, and sink her in open water before the river freezes and she walks away over the ice.

**Rolls and what they became:**

| Roll | Result | Became |
|---|---|---|
| SET 33 | Plague-era river town | Harrow's Crossing under fever quarantine; a Chapel ward, a Mill yard, a command post |
| ROLE 39 | Drone fleet operator | One Sparrow recon quad (jobs, scouting) and one Cormorant strike drone |
| LOOP 23 | Repair a failing machine piece by piece | Cormorant has four slots (Airframe, Optics, Datalink, Payload); each buy-and-fit is a turn |
| CLOCK 8 | Winter closes the passes | The ice gauge: starts 10, drops on hidden rolls, river freezes at 0 |
| TWIST 18 | Two currencies, shifting rate | Scrip (Command pay, Chapel, munitions) vs silver (Mill parts); rate drifts every turn |
| WIN 4 | Destroy a named enemy | Sink the barge Marigold |
| MAP 7 | Three islands and the sea between | Ferry, Mill, Chapel islands; two docks and four reaches form the Marigold's circuit |
| TONE 6 | Military procedural | ROE (no strikes at a dock), requisitions, fixes, a stern note from Command |

Signature detail: the ice gauge chalked on the shed wall, a visible clock with hidden drop odds.

**Rulesheet (verbatim):**

1. One action per turn: Scout an island with the Sparrow, Take a posted job, Buy and fit one Cormorant part, or Strike. Swapping scrip and silver at the posted rate is free, any turn, whole silver only.
2. Command pays you 6 scrip at the end of every turn. Mill Island sells for silver only, Chapel Island for scrip only, Command requisitions for scrip. The rate moves every turn.
3. The Cormorant strike drone needs Airframe and Payload to fly a strike. Optics +1 and Datalink +1 to the strike roll; Datalink also shows the Marigold's position every turn.
4. Strike: roll d6 + bonuses, need 5+. A hit sinks the Marigold and wins. A miss spends the Payload; on a 1-2 the airframe cracks too.
5. The Marigold moves on a circuit of two docks and four reaches. You may only strike her in a reach; if she is docked when you launch, the strike aborts and the turn is lost.
6. Scouting an island reveals its dock and the reaches beside it. If the Sparrow finds her in a reach, roll d6: on a 1 her crew shoots it down and you can never scout or run jobs again.
7. You may strip the Sparrow's camera to fit Optics for free, but then the Sparrow is gone for good.
8. The ice gauge starts at 10 and drops some turns. At 0 the river freezes, the Marigold walks off over the ice, and you lose. Turn 18 is the hard stop.

## Playthrough

The player spent the first three turns running Sparrow jobs for silver and scrip, including a 12 Ag packet run with a one-in-six chance of losing the drone (roll 5, safe), while watching the silver rate climb from 2.00 to 3.00. On turn 4 they bought the Airframe, and on turn 5 chose the 20-scrip Datalink over munitions, reasoning that a permanent position feed was worth more than blind scouting; the Marigold immediately showed up in the South Reach. Munitions followed on turn 6, but the barge then sat at Mill Dock for two turns, so the player banked two scrip jobs and waited. On turn 9 she moved into the Backwater and the player took a 50 percent shot rather than spend the silver dip on Optics: roll 3 +1 = 4, a miss, and the airframe check came up 1, cracking the spar. That left Scrip 42, Silver 4, two parts to rebuy and the ice at 6 with eight turns left. The player checked status, ran the risky packet job again (roll 2, safe), then on turn 11 skipped the fire-watch job and swapped 24 scrip for 8 silver to buy the Airframe without spending a turn. Munitions came on turn 12 with exactly 30 scrip in hand, the same night a hard freeze dropped the ice gauge two points to 2. On turn 13, with the Marigold in the South Reach, the strike rolled 6 +1 = 7 and sank her.

Result: WIN on turn 13 of 18. Final numbers: Scrip 6, Silver 0, rate 3.50, Ice 2, Cormorant Air OK / Opt -- / Link OK / Pay spent, Sparrow intact.

## Rules fit

- Rulesheet: 8 lines, at the cap. Several lines are long; line 6 does double duty (scout coverage and the shoot-down risk).
- Tracked numbers: three player resources (scrip, silver, Cormorant parts list). The status line also shows the exchange rate and the ice gauge, which are world numbers the player does not spend. That is defensible under the letter of the rule but the status line carries five numbers plus a four-slot checklist, which is dense.
- One decision per turn: held throughout. The free currency swap is a sub-step, not a second decision.
- Status line: same format every turn.
- Turn length: all turns under 180 words.
- Invented mid-game: nothing that stopped play. Whole-silver rounding on swaps was already in rule 1. The strike option was labeled with its percentage as a courtesy, not a rule. Job pay amounts were GM-chosen from a short list rather than rolled; only the job type (none / scrip / silver) was rolled.

## Decisions

Thirteen turns played.

- Genuinely hard: 5 (T3 risky packet run vs safe play; T5 Datalink vs munitions first; T7 strip the Sparrow vs keep the job income; T9 strike at 50 percent now vs buy Optics on the silver dip; T11 job vs currency swap to save a turn).
- Obvious: 8 (T1, T2, T4, T6, T8, T10, T12, T13 were each the only sensible move given the numbers; T13 was forced by the ice).

## Emergence

1. The silver rate ran from 2.00 to 3.25 over the first five turns, pricing the Optics (18 Ag) out of reach in scrip terms and quietly steering the player toward the scrip-priced Datalink.
2. The Marigold dwelt at Mill Dock two turns running, so a loaded, ready Cormorant had to sit idle while the player ran census and medicine jobs.
3. The rate dipped to 2.25 on exactly the turn she entered open water, creating a real buy-the-dip-vs-shoot-now dilemma.
4. The miss followed by the airframe crack (two bad rolls) wiped out 54 scrip-equivalent of parts and turned the back half into a comeback.
5. The ice gauge sat at 8 for five turns and then dropped 1, 1, 1, 1, 2 in the last five, so the pressure arrived all at once just as the rebuild finished.
6. Datalink made the Sparrow redundant for scouting but not for income, which is why the strip-for-Optics option was never taken: a synergy the rules did not state but the systems produced.

Count: 6.

## Problems

- Scouting was never used. Datalink at 20 scrip is cheap enough that it obsoletes the Sparrow's scouting role by turn 5; three of the five option slots on early turns were dead weight. Either Datalink should cost more, show position only every other turn, or scouting should offer something Datalink does not (for example reading where she goes next).
- Jobs were repetitive: six job templates cycled with no escalation and no interaction with the Marigold or the ice. SimCopter-style jobs should come from world state (a fire at the Mill when she docks there, a medevac when the ice drops).
- Only two of six positions are docks, so strike windows were frequent (four of the seven turns after the first fix). The ROE constraint rarely bit.
- The ice gauge barely moved for five turns, so the clock had no teeth until turn 9. A guaranteed minimum drop every other turn would keep it honest.
- Optics and the Sparrow-strip rule never came into play; two of eight rule lines did no work this session.
- Loss-streak compensation is absent. After the miss the comeback was feasible only because the rate and job rolls cooperated; a hazard-pay bump after a miss would make the squeeze fairer.
- Events (rate roll of 6) never fired, so the event deck went unseen.
- Tone: the fever setting is mostly wallpaper. The game would play identically as a smuggler hunt without the plague.

## Fun rating

7 out of 10. The economy did real work: every turn had arithmetic worth doing, the rate swings changed which part I could afford, and the turn-11 swap-instead-of-job move felt like finding an exploit in my favor. It loses points because the information game collapsed once Datalink was bought and the jobs were filler between strike windows rather than a system of their own.

## Would replay

Yes, once or twice. I want to try the Optics-first line (buy silver on a dip, strike at 67 percent) and see whether the Marigold's dwell rolls punish patience the way they rewarded it here. It would need the scouting and job fixes above to earn a third play.
