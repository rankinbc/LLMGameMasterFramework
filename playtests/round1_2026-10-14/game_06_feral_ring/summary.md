# Feral Ring - playtest summary (game 06)

## Game

**Title:** Feral Ring

**Premise:** The Kepler-442c survey ring is six sealed sectors on a loop. Native magnetotactic grazers have chewed through the hull and are eating the superconducting bus. You are a beast tamer with a dart gun and a stock of Serum; so is Kessler, who runs the kennel on the far side of the ring. The Survey Board will hand the ring to whoever holds more sectors when the shuttle lands on turn 16, or the moment the ring cracks, whichever comes first. A roaming Bull is worth two sectors and cannot be sabotaged. Every feral left loose chews Integrity, so every harness cut to hurt your rival also brings the breach closer.

**Rolls:** `SET=38 ROLE=30 LOOP=14 CLOCK=5 TWIST=25 WIN=6 MAP=8 TONE=4`

| Roll | Table result | Became |
|---|---|---|
| SET 38 | Monster-infested hinterland with villages | Six sealed sectors of a survey ring overrun by native grazers |
| ROLE 30 | Beast tamer | Tamer with Serum darts and harnesses; one beast per pen holds a sector |
| LOOP 14 | Chase a target that moves each turn | The Bull, moving one sector a turn on a d6, worth double |
| CLOCK 5 | Hull or structure integrity decays | Integrity 50, falling 1 + ferals loose each turn; 0 ends the game |
| TWIST 25 | You can sabotage instead of compete | Sabotage cuts Kessler's harness; beast goes feral, anyone can re-tame it, clock speeds up |
| WIN 6 | Hold the most nodes when the clock ends | Most sectors at shuttle or breach; ties to Kessler |
| MAP 8 | Single location with 6 districts | Ring A-F, one pen per sector |
| TONE 4 | Hard sci-fi | Magnetotactic grazers eating the bus, induction collars, hull plates |

Signature detail: the Bull's harness-snap charge (never fired this game), and Kessler as a rival who plays by exactly the player's rules with a fixed, hidden priority list.

**Rulesheet (verbatim):**

```
1. Six sectors in a loop: A Hydroponics (your lab), B Reactor, C Habitat, D Kennels (Kessler's), E Workshop, F Airlock. Each turn: move to an adjacent sector or stay, then take one action there.
2. Tame (1 Serum, roll 4+): harness a feral in your sector. You hold the sector while your beast stands in its pen. One pen per sector.
3. Sabotage (1 Serum, roll 4+): cut the harness on Kessler's beast in your sector. It goes feral and the sector is nobody's until someone tames it.
4. Integrity drops every turn by 1 plus the number of feral beasts loose. Ferals chew the bus.
5. The Bull roams one sector a turn. Taming it costs 2 Serum and a 5+. Its sector counts double and cannot be sabotaged. If it walks into a harnessed beast, roll 1-2 and that harness snaps.
6. Serum: +1 per sector you hold at the start of each turn.
7. Kessler plays by the same rules every turn. You see what she did.
8. Game ends after turn 16 or the moment Integrity hits 0. Most sectors wins; ties go to Kessler.
```

Goal: hold more sectors than Kessler when the ring is counted. Lose if she holds as many or more. Turns: 16.

## Playthrough

The opening dart at the Reactor feral missed, then the Bull wandered into the Reactor on its own and the player spent two Serum on a 5+ and hit it, going up 3 to 1 on turn 2. Rather than tame the loose Reactor feral to slow the clock, the player reasoned that a fast clock favours the leader and went on the offensive, cutting Kessler's kennel harness at D on turn 4, only to watch her step in from next door and re-tame it the same turn. That started a four-turn tug of war in the same kennel: the player cut or tamed first each turn, Kessler answered at 50 percent, and the sector changed hands five times while the Airlock breached and a third feral chewed the bus. The player broke off, took the free Airlock feral at F for a 4 to 2 lead on turn 9, then traded whiffs with Kessler as they swapped positions around the ring. Turn 12 both landed cuts in the same turn, four ferals went loose, and Integrity dropped to 10. Turn 13 the player re-took D for 4 to 2, but a second grazer breached into the Reactor beside the Bull, and turn 14 the player missed the Habitat feral on a 1 while Kessler's route to that same feral took her through the unguarded lab at A, where she cut the runner on a 6. With Integrity at 1 the final turn was a straight race: the player needed a 4+ at C and rolled 2; Kessler needed a 4+ at A and rolled 4. Final count 3 to 3, Bull counting double, and the tiebreak gave the ring to Kessler.

Result: loss (tie, awarded to Kessler), game ended by breach at the end of turn 15 of 16. Final numbers: Integrity 0, Serum 20, held B (Bull, 2) + D = 3 versus Kessler A, E, F = 3.

## Rules fit

Within caps. Rulesheet 8 lines. Three tracked numbers: Integrity, Serum, held-sector list. Kessler's holdings, both positions, the Bull's position and the feral list also appear in the status line, but those are world and enemy state the player perceives, not numbers the player manages. One decision per turn with 3 to 4 options. Same status format every turn. Longest turn was about 150 words. Turn budget stated up front (16); game ended at 15, inside the 12 to 25 band.

Three things got clarified or decided during play:
- "One pen per sector" was on the rulesheet, but its consequence (taming a feral in a sector you already hold adds no sector, only slows the chew) was explained in one line on turn 3.
- The sabotage-then-retame combo (a cut beast is just a feral, whoever darts it next owns it) was explained on turn 4 after it bit the player. It follows directly from rules 2 and 3 but was not obvious until it happened.
- Kessler's hidden policy needed one GM ruling on turn 14: when her move toward a target lands her in a sector with a player beast, she evaluates her tame/sabotage priorities in the new sector (move then act, same as the player). That ruling is consistent with rule 7 but was made at the table.

Nothing in the rulesheet was contradicted. The Bull's snap rule (line 5, second half) never triggered because the Bull was tamed on turn 2.

## Decisions

Genuinely hard: 6 (turns 2, 3, 5, 8, 10, 11). Obvious or forced: 9 (turns 1, 4, 6, 7, 9, 12, 13, 14, 15).

The hard ones were real: Bull gamble versus safe feral (T2), slow the clock versus go on offence (T3, in hindsight the game-deciding choice), keep trading cuts in the kennel or reposition (T5, T8), guard a pen or attack (T10, T11). Turns 6, 7, 12, 13 were the same action with a different number, and the last two turns had exactly one sensible play.

## Emergence

Nine moments where the systems produced something unplanned:

1. T1: the Bull's random walk took it into the player's sector right after the failed opening dart, turning a planned chase into a one-turn gamble.
2. T4: Kessler re-tamed the kennel beast the same turn it was cut, revealing that sabotage next to the rival is a gift to her.
3. T5: the Airlock breach landed in the middle of the kennel fight, adding a third feral while both tamers were far from it.
4. T6-T7: the D tug of war, with the player acting first every turn and both sides at 50 percent, became a loop neither side wanted to leave.
5. T9: Kessler abandoned D to follow the player around the ring, which is what let the Airlock feral be taken uncontested.
6. T10-T11: the two tamers swapped sectors, each standing on the other's pen, and both missed twice.
7. T12: both cuts landed in the same turn, four ferals went loose, and the clock jumped from 3 to 5 a turn.
8. T13: the second Reactor breach put two chewers beside the untouchable Bull, so the player's strongest sector became the biggest Integrity drain.
9. T14: Kessler's shortest route to the Habitat feral ran through the player's lab, and the policy that was chasing a feral cut the runner on the way, setting up the tiebreak loss.

## Problems

- Serum was never scarce. The player finished with 20 and Kessler with 12; after turn 3 the economy number did nothing. Either sabotage should cost 2 or 3, income should be flat 1 instead of per sector, or Serum needs a second sink (buy a hull patch, pay for a re-roll). As played, one of the three tracked numbers was dead weight.
- Every action is a flat 4+ coin flip with no way to improve odds. No upgrade, no counter, no loadout. The taste profile wants mastery; this game offered positioning and timing but the dice did most of the deciding, and the mid-game read as a sequence of coin flips.
- Turns 5 to 8 were repetitive: the same kennel, the same beast, the same option list three turns running. Kessler's policy (tame the feral in her sector first) made her stand still, which is correct play for her but dull to fight.
- The LOOP roll (chase a moving target) was short-circuited by luck on turn 1 and never came back. With a different Bull roll the game would have had a real chase; as played the core verb lasted one turn.
- The "fast clock suits the leader" line of play was genuinely wrong, which is good design, but the player could not see Kessler's Serum or route, so the lesson only arrived in hindsight. A visible Kessler Serum number or a one-line tell about where she is heading would make it a readable trade-off rather than a trap.
- Tie to Kessler was stated on the rulesheet and was fair, but losing on the tiebreak after the player's 3-to-2 lead dissolved on back-to-back rolls of 1 and 2 felt like variance rather than consequence.
- Difficulty was about right: the player led for 13 of 15 turns and lost on the last two rolls, so the result was close, but the margin was dice, not decisions.

## Fun rating

6 out of 10. The Integrity clock tied to the feral count was one real interacting system, and it bit at exactly the right moment: leaving one feral loose for fourteen turns cost the one extra turn that would have won. But after the Bull gamble on turn 2 every turn was the same 1-Serum 4+ roll, the Serum pile meant nothing, and the four-turn kennel fight was a coin-flip loop I could not play better, only leave.

## Would replay

Yes, once. There is a clear lesson to test (dart the Reactor feral early, never cut a harness Kessler can reach next turn, keep a body near A in the last five turns), and a different Bull roll on turn 1 would make the chase an actual chase. I would want sabotage to cost 2 Serum and a Serum sink added before a second run, or the economy number should be dropped and the slot given to something that changes the odds.
