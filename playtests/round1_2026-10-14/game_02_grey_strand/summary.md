# The Grey Strand - playtest summary

## Game

**Title:** The Grey Strand

**Premise:** You are admiral of the last four ships of a drowned sea-kingdom, the Strand: a line of ten harbors owned by three factions (Harbor Lords, Fisher Clans, Tidewardens). The Grey Tide, a plague of the sea that raises drowned things, holds the northern end and walks south one harbor at a time whenever your line breaks or you give ground. Your carrier is a roc-ship: three proud, living rocs are your air wing, and every launch costs their Trust. Each turn a wave breaks on your line; you choose how to meet it, whether to do the local faction's task, or whether to trade a harbor for rest. Win by getting two of the three factions to pledge their crowns, which lets the Rite turn the Tide.

**Rolls and what they became:**
- SET 23 Carrier strike group at sea -> a roc-ship (Thornhal) and three escorts
- ROLE 5 Fleet admiral with 4 ships -> four ships, one Hull pool (every 3 Hull lost sinks one)
- LOOP 7 Hold a line against waves, repair between -> wave vs line each turn; repair and fall-back as the "between"
- CLOCK 7 Plague spreads one node per turn -> the Grey Tide takes a harbor on every breach or fallback; loss when it reaches harbor 10
- TWIST 27 One resource is sentient and resists being used -> the rocs and their Trust meter; at 0 one leaves for good
- WIN 8 Majority with three factions -> two of three crowns at Favor 5
- MAP 1 Linear track of 10 stops -> ten named harbors, fleet position = Tide + 1
- TONE 5 Mythic -> Tide, Rite, crowns, rocs, omens
- Signature detail: Skarra, the eldest roc, who bargains through the ship's seer

**Rulesheet (verbatim):**
1. The Strand is ten harbors in a line. The Tide holds harbor 1; your fleet holds the line at the next harbor south. Every turn a wave breaks on your line.
2. Wave = d6 + half the turn number (rounded up). Line = 2 per ship afloat + 2 per roc you launch. If the wave beats the line, Hull loses the difference and the Tide takes your harbor. Hold by 6 or more and the harbor's faction gives +1 Favor.
3. Hull starts 12; every 3 lost sinks a ship. Repairing in harbor restores 3 Hull, but docked ships count 1 each in the line that turn.
4. Your 3 rocs are living and proud. Each roc launched costs 1 Trust; a turn with none launched gains 1 (max 6). At Trust 0 one roc leaves for good and Trust resets to 2. Fisher Clan tasks feed them.
5. Three factions own the harbors: Harbor Lords (2, 5, 8), Fisher Clans (3, 6, 9), Tidewardens (4, 7, 10). Favor runs 0 to 5; at 5 a faction pledges its crown.
6. Fall back one harbor: no wave this turn, Hull +3, Trust +2, but the Tide takes the harbor you left.
7. Each harbor offers one task when you reach it. Doing it is your action for the turn (ships hold alone, rocs rest) and pays Favor on a roll.
8. Win: two crowns pledged, and the Rite turns the Tide. Lose: the Tide reaches Lanthorn (harbor 10), Hull hits 0, or turn 20 ends without two crowns.

## Playthrough

The player opened at Gullmere by ferrying the Lords' silver while the wave was still too small to break a ships-only line, then spent one roc on a failed bid for a hold-by-6. A refugee-boat event on turn 3 offered a free Lords point if the ships held alone, and the wave came in at exactly 8 against a line of 8, a tie that held. On turn 4 the player launched all three rocs for a guaranteed hold-by-6 and the Harbor Lords pledged their crown, one of two, with Trust down to 2. The fleet fell back to Netcombe, towed the Fisher Clans' net-boats clear (Fisher 3, rocs fed to the cap), then burned all three rocs on a 67 percent shot at another point and held by 5, one short. With Trust at 3 and nothing left at Netcombe, the player fell back three turns in a row through Saltspire and Coinwater, skipping the Wardens' bell and the already-pledged Lords' barge job, to reach Hookhaven, the Fisher Clans' great harbor. There the player spent capped Trust on a safe all-roc line (no point), then on turn 12 did the clans' last great catch with ships alone: the wave broke the line by 1, Hull dropped to 11 and the Tide took Hookhaven, but the catch roll succeeded and the Fisher Clans pledged the second crown in the same turn. Result: win on turn 12 of 20. Final numbers: Hull 11/12 (4 ships), Trust 6/6 (3 rocs), Favor Lords 5, Fisher 5, Wardens 1, Tide at harbor 6 of 10. Twelve turns played plus one status query.

## Rules fit

- Rulesheet: 8 lines, at the cap. Every line is long; a stricter reading of "8 lines" would call some of them two sentences each.
- Tracked numbers: Hull, Trust, Favor (three factions under one label with one rule). Within the cap of 3, though the status line also carries the Tide position and ship count, so a player sees six numbers plus the turn.
- One decision per turn, 3 to 5 numbered options every turn, status line in the same format every turn, dice shown on every roll, every GM turn under 180 words. All held.
- Turn budget 20 stated up front; the game ended at 12, the floor of the 12 to 25 range.
- Rulings invented mid-game (none contradicted the sheet, but none were on it): a wave equal to the line holds; a task still resolves when the line breaks that turn; the refugee escort pays only if the line holds; the hidden Hookhaven task's "feed either way" clause. Rule 3 (repair) was never used because Hull never dropped below 11, so repair-plus-launch combinations were never tested.
- Design note: before presenting, the GM tuned the margin threshold (4 to 6) and roc line value (3 to 2) after paper arithmetic showed a two-crown win was reachable from the first harbor; no dice had been consumed.

## Decisions

Twelve decision turns. Six had a genuinely hard choice, six were obvious.

- Hard (6): T2 (one roc, all rocs, or move on with Lords at 3), T4 (burn 3 Trust for a guaranteed crown or save it), T7 (spend capped Trust on a 67 percent shot or fall back), T9 (commit to the Fisher plan vs the Wardens plan, skip the bell), T11 (order of operations: spend Trust before the catch refills it), T12 (do the task now at an 83 percent breach risk or fall back first).
- Obvious (6): T1 (task is free when the wave cannot break), T3 (refugee escort was a guaranteed point), T5 (fall back from a tapped-out harbor), T6 (task with a one-in-six risk), T8 (fall back; Trust too low for anything else), T10 (fall back from a dead harbor).

## Emergence

Five moments where the systems produced something unplanned:

1. T3: the refugee event landed on a turn where the max wave exactly equalled the line, turning a "risky escort" into a guaranteed point, and then the wave rolled a 6 and tied the line, holding by zero.
2. T4: launching all three rocs for a guaranteed crown made the Trust cost real for the first time (Trust 2, "the rocs will not be touched"), which drove the first fallback.
3. T7: an all-roc launch held by exactly 5 when 6 was needed, wasting three Trust and forcing a retreat with nothing earned; the one-short miss was the most felt beat of the game.
4. T9 to T10: once the Lords were pledged, Coinwater became dead space, and the Trust cap meant the fallback bonuses were wasted, so the clock was spent on pure positioning. An unintended "pledged factions make their harbors worthless" dynamic.
5. T12: the line broke and Hookhaven fell to the Tide in the same turn the clans' catch pledged the crown and won the game; the win and the loss of the harbor resolved together.

The sea event deck fired only twice (turns 1 and 3) in twelve turns, so from turn 4 on all surprises came from the dice-versus-threshold math, not events.

## Problems

- Too easy. Hull lost 1 point all game; no ship ever sank; repair was never worth offering. The loss conditions were never in sight (Tide at 6 of 10 at the end, four harbors of slack).
- The Lords' crown was pledged by turn 4 from a start of 1 Favor: task +2, event +1, guaranteed margin +1. Starting Favor should be 0, or the margin bonus should be harder to buy with rocs.
- Fall back is too generous (no wave, Hull +3, Trust +2 with no downside but the clock). It became the default whenever a harbor was tapped out, producing three consecutive low-decision turns (8 to 10). It needs a cost, for example -1 Favor with the abandoned harbor's faction, or only one of the two bonuses.
- A pledged faction's remaining harbors have nothing to offer. Their tasks should pay something else once pledged (Hull, Trust, or a one-time wave reduction).
- Trust capping at 6 wasted two fallback bonuses and a rest; it rewarded "spend before you refill" ordering, which was a good decision, but the cap was hit too often.
- The 20-turn budget was far longer than needed; a 12-turn win felt like leaving the second half of the board (Candlehold, Lordsgate, Lastnet, Lanthorn and the whole Wardens path) unplayed.
- Events at one-in-three per turn still went quiet for nine straight turns; the refugee event is a free point whenever the wave cannot reach the line.
- Minor: a task succeeding while the harbor falls is a touch odd on paper, though it produced the best moment of the game.

## Fun rating

**6 / 10.** The core arithmetic was genuinely engaging: every turn I was computing the breach threshold, the hold-by-6 odds and the Trust refill timing, and the "spend capped Trust before the feed" play felt like a real optimization I found rather than one the game handed me. But the pressure never arrived: Hull was decorative, one crown came almost free, and the middle stretch of fall-back turns was a procession rather than a game.

## Would replay

Yes, once, with the fall-back cost and starting Favor fixed. The Trust-versus-Favor-versus-clock triangle is a real decision engine, and the untouched Wardens path (bell, beacons, a harder roll-gated climb) is an obvious second route to try; without the tuning, a second play would be the same procession.
