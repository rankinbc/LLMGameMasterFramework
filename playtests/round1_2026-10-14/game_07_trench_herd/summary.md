# Trench Herd - playtest summary (game 07)

## Game

**Title:** Trench Herd

**Premise:** You run the submersible and the tamed animals on Station Kettle, a company mining platform in a polar trench. Each turn you dive one of four sites (paying battery Charge), draw an encounter from that site's hidden deck, and roll 2d6 to see how much the platform's Stores grow. The crew burns 2 Stores a turn, the surface ice closes the shallow sites one by one as winter sets in, and the icebreaker that collects everyone arrives at the end of turn 16. A sealed company envelope opens at the halfway mark.

**Rolls:** SET=34 ROLE=30 LOOP=2 CLOCK=8 TWIST=35 WIN=14 MAP=10 TONE=2

| Roll | Table result | Became |
|---|---|---|
| SET 34 | Deep-sea mining platform | Station Kettle, a polar-trench mining platform |
| ROLE 30 | Beast tamer | The Herd: tame crabs, mantas, an octopus, lantern eels; each gives +2 at its home site and eats 1 Stores per turn |
| LOOP 2 | Pick a destination, pay fuel, resolve what is there | Pick a dive site, pay Charge, draw an encounter, roll 2d6 |
| CLOCK 8 | Winter closes the passes | Ice closes one site for good on turns 6, 9, 12, shallowest first |
| TWIST 35 | Hidden objective revealed at halfway | Directive 7-C on turn 8: a live Lantern Eel is required for pickup |
| WIN 14 | Keep a settlement above a threshold until the end | Stores 12 or more when the icebreaker docks after turn 16 |
| MAP 10 | No map; a deck of encounters | Four sites, each a d6 deck of encounters, no travel graph |
| TONE 2 | Dry humor | Company memos, Okonkwo's mug, the Landlord |

Signature detail: the Herd (beasts as site-specific multipliers with upkeep), plus the sleeping Leviathan the crew calls the Landlord.

**Rulesheet (verbatim):**

    1. Each turn: dive one site (pay its Charge, draw an encounter, roll 2d6 plus bonuses) or stay home and recharge (+6 Charge).
    2. Charge is the sub's battery. You cannot dive a site you cannot pay for.
    3. Stores feed and heat the platform. The crew burns 2 Stores every turn. Stores at 0 means evacuation: you lose.
    4. Herd: tamed beasts, max 3. Each gives +2 on all rolls at its home site and eats 1 Stores per turn. Release one any time, free.
    5. Taming: when a beast is drawn, roll 2d6, 9+ tames it. Failure costs Charge.
    6. Winter: the ice closes one site for good on turns 6, 9 and 12, shallowest first. The Lantern Shelf never freezes.
    7. A sealed company envelope opens on turn 8.
    8. Win: Stores at 12 or more when the icebreaker docks at the end of turn 16.

Tracked numbers: Charge, Stores, Herd (list with rules). Start: Charge 6, Stores 14, Herd none.

## Playthrough

The opening went well: two Nodule Plain dives and two Vent Garden dives took Stores from 14 to 22 by turn 4 on a mix of good and mediocre rolls, with the battery drained to zero in the process. After the first recharge the player gambled on the Lantern Shelf on turn 6, reasoning that the one site that never freezes was the place to establish a beast early; an eel was drawn and the tame roll (4, need 9+) failed, costing 2 Charge and a forced recharge turn. The envelope on turn 8 then made a live eel mandatory for pickup, which retroactively justified the gamble and also locked the player into the Shelf. A second eel tame failed by one (8). With the Nodule Plain frozen, the player asked about tame odds, learned that clam bait from the now-frozen Vent Garden or a galley find at the Wreck would have given +2, and spent two turns at the Wreck: a galley miss (+3, no bait) and a 12 on the collapsed deck (+6) that briefly pushed Stores back to 17. The final Shelf-only phase was an alternation of forced recharges and single dives: the Leviathan woke on a 9 (need 10+), raising the Shelf cost to 5, and the last eel of the game rolled snake eyes. By turn 14 the player could see that one remaining dive could not deliver both the eel and 12 Stores and played it out for the eel anyway. Result: loss. Final numbers: Stores 9, Charge 3, Herd none. 16 turns played.

## Rules fit

Within caps: 8 rule lines, 3 tracked numbers (Charge, Stores, Herd), one decision per turn, status line in the same format every turn, every turn under 180 words, dice shown each time. The "Open: ..." list in the status line is world state rather than a tracked number but it does add reading load.

Nothing had to be invented mid-game. Two pieces of information were in the hidden facts from the start and surfaced through play as the skill intends: the heater conversion (3 Charge for 2 Stores, revealed on turn 5) and bait (+2 on the next tame, revealed when the player asked on turn 10). One small ambiguity: rule 6 says the ice closes a site "on" turn 6, 9, 12; the GM resolved that as "after the dive on that turn," announced a turn ahead. That worked but should be in the rule text. The Leviathan's +1 Charge surcharge was a deck effect, explained in one line when it fired, and fit the skill's rule about new information arriving through play.

## Decisions

- Genuinely hard: 4 (turn 6 Shelf gamble vs Wreck vs last Vent dive; turn 10 Wreck for bait and cells vs straight to the Shelf; turn 11 second Wreck dive at Charge 3 vs recharge; turn 13 dive vs bank Charge).
- Moderate, with a reasonable default: 6 (turns 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 15).
- Obvious or forced: 6 (turns 5, 7, 9, 12, 14, 16, all recharge turns with Charge at 0 or 1).

Six forced turns out of sixteen is too many. Every dive drains the battery to within one dive of empty and the +6 recharge only funds one or two dives, so the game settles into dive, recharge, dive, recharge.

## Emergence

1. Turn 6: the Vent Garden froze and its crabs migrated to the Nodule Plain, changing what that deck could offer (never used, but the player noticed).
2. Turn 8: the directive landed one turn after the player had already gambled on the Shelf and lost; the twist and the player's own plan collided in a way that felt earned rather than scripted.
3. Turn 10: the bait answer revealed that the eel objective had a prerequisite at the shallowest site, which had frozen four turns earlier. A real dependency chain the player discovered too late.
4. Turn 11: a 12 on the collapsed deck turned a desperate second Wreck dive into the best haul of the game, briefly reopening a win line.
5. Turn 13: waking the Landlord raised the Shelf cost from 4 to 5, so a +6 recharge now bought exactly one dive, hard-locking the endgame rhythm.
6. Turn 15: snake eyes on the last eel.

Count: 6, of which 3 (crab migration, bait-after-the-freeze, Landlord surcharge) were systems interacting and 3 were dice swings with narrative weight.

## Problems

- The Charge economy is too tight. Starting Charge 6 and a +6 recharge against site costs of 1 to 4 means roughly 40 percent of turns are forced recharges with nothing to decide. Recharge should give more, or dives should be cheaper, or there should be a second thing to do on a platform turn.
- The hidden objective is a 28 percent roll with no reliable way to improve it. Taming at 9+ unassisted, with the only bait sources at sites that freeze, made the win depend on a roll the player could only attempt about four times all game. Three attempts, three failures is unlucky (roughly one in three) but the structure made that outcome common. Either tame at 8+, make bait reliably available, or let the directive accept an alternative (any beast, or a specimen sample at lower value).
- The Herd mechanic never engaged. The player ended with zero beasts, so rule 4 and the upkeep tension were dead weight in this run. Beast draws are 1 in 6 at most sites and then 28 percent to tame, so an empty Herd is the expected result.
- Lategame with only the Shelf open is repetitive: dive, flat, recharge, dive. The winter clock removes options without adding any. A site that opens when the ice forms (an under-ice shelf, say) would keep the choice count up.
- Stores never approached zero, so the stated lose condition did nothing; the real failure was missing the threshold.
- Difficulty: too hard as tuned. Estimated win rate with competent play is well under half, driven mostly by the eel roll.

Good parts worth keeping: the shallow-first freeze gives a clean planning horizon, the deck-per-site structure makes each site feel different, the humor landed without taking space, and the player could always see the arithmetic.

## Fun rating

4 / 10. The first six turns had the right feel, numbers to plan around and a real gamble on turn 6, but from turn 7 onward the game narrowed to a single 28 percent roll I could attempt only every other turn, and nothing I did changed those odds. I like being squeezed by a system; I do not like being told the whole game is one die.

## Would replay

No, not as tuned. With tame at 8+, a bigger recharge, and a reason to keep diving the shallow sites before they freeze (bait, crabs that matter), the skeleton is good and I would try it again; the deck-per-site design and the freeze schedule are worth another pass.
