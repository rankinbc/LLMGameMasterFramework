# Taste Profile

Prompt input for an AI game master. Read this before inventing a new, lightweight text game for this player. The player's favorite games are the 11 below. The profile extracts what they share and what makes each one fun, so the GM can build something new in the same spirit rather than imitate any one of them.

## The 11 games

- **Counter-Strike 1.6** (2003, round-based tactical shooter) - a persistent money economy across rounds: win and buy big, lose and eco-save, every round's outcome changes next round's purchasing power.
- **Cities: Skylines** (2015, city builder) - a build-expand-optimize flywheel where each solution (more roads, more zoning) creates the next visible problem (traffic), and population is the one big number that unlocks everything.
- **Escape Velocity** (1996, 2D space trader/combat sandbox) - total freedom across three pillars (trade, missions, combat) with per-faction reputation that opens and closes doors; shuttle-to-warship power fantasy.
- **F/A-18 Hornet 2.0** (1995, carrier flight sim) - the sortie as a complete arc: brief, pick loadout against fuel/range, transit through a populated theater, a sharp 5-minute engagement, then the earned return and carrier trap.
- **The Guardian Legend** (1988, NES exploration + shoot-em-up hybrid) - prepare in the overworld (farm chips, buy stat upgrades), perform in a short intense corridor, with one shared resource pool so overworld spending matters in the corridor.
- **RPGWO** (c.2000, persistent sandbox MMO) - no quests at all; gather, craft, trade, fight, build on a persistent map where multi-stage crafting chains and player shops create a real economy and player-set goals.
- **SimCopter** (1996, helicopter emergency-response sim) - jobs pop up on the radio from a living city (fires, wrecks, riots, medevac); pick which to take, earn cash, upgrade to a better chopper, move to a harder city.
- **StarCraft** (1998, real-time strategy) - mine, spend, scout, counter, expand, under fog of war; a counter matrix with no dominant strategy, three asymmetric races, and a supply cap that forces decisive battles.
- **Stranded Deep** (2015, ocean survival) - short-term meters (thirst, hunger, sun) pull against long-term escape goals; raft-based expeditions from a home island with a tool-gated crafting ladder.
- **The Magic of Scheherazade** (1989, NES action/turn-based RPG hybrid) - Arabian Nights chapters with recruited allies, shared MP across two combat modes, and past/future time travel that changes what is open where.
- **Warcraft II** (1995, real-time strategy) - gold plus lumber with finite mines that deplete and force expansion; boom-vs-rush tension; a tech ladder (Town Hall to Keep to Castle) that gates the power units.

## Mechanics this player enjoys

Each of these can be dropped into a 10-30 turn chat game.

- A money economy that carries across rounds: win big and spend, lose and save for a later force-buy (CS16).
- Loss-streak compensation so the trailing side is never fully dead, only squeezed (CS16).
- Buy low, sell high across a map of nodes with different prices; cargo capacity as the limiter (EV).
- Per-faction reputation on a numeric scale; thresholds open missions, close ports, trigger hostility (EV).
- Upgrade ladder for the player's one vessel: ship, chopper, jet loadout, raft, tool tier (EV, SimCopter, SD, F/A-18).
- Finite resource deposits that deplete and force expansion into contested ground (WC2, StarCraft).
- Two resources where one is for basics and one gates advanced options (WC2 gold/lumber, SC minerals/gas).
- Tech tree gating: building or research prerequisites unlock stronger units or options (WC2, SC, C:S milestones).
- Supply/food cap that limits army size and forces fights instead of infinite stockpiling (WC2, SC).
- Fog of war with cheap scouting actions; guessing the opponent's plan and countering it (SC, WC2).
- Rock-paper-scissors counter matrix with no dominant composition (SC, WC2).
- Boom vs rush: invest in economy now or strike before the opponent is ready (WC2, SC).
- Timing windows: a one-way-door all-in when a specific upgrade lands (SC).
- Asymmetric sides or factions that play differently, ideally with a side switch at the half (CS16, SC).
- Population or score as a single visible progress number that unlocks tiers (C:S).
- Every solution creates the next problem; optimization feeds growth feeds new bottlenecks (C:S).
- Budget sliders and tax rates: tune a few percentages and watch income, demand, and coverage respond (C:S).
- Milestone unlocks that add new problems, not just new toys (C:S).
- Short-term survival meters that decay (thirst, fuel, health) competing with long-term goals (SD, F/A-18).
- Risk/reward expeditions from a safe base; carry supplies out, bring loot back, process it (SD, TGL, SimCopter).
- Multi-stage crafting chains: ore to bar to sword; each stage needs a tool from the previous (RPGWO, SD).
- Tool-gated access: cannot reach the next island without a raft, cannot build a raft without an axe (SD).
- Prepare-then-perform rhythm: a calm buildup phase, then a short intense test, then assess (TGL, F/A-18, CS16).
- One shared resource pool across modes so spending in one context costs you in the other (TGL, TMOS).
- Permanent, visible clears: a corridor done, a mine captured, a plane part found; progress never regresses (TGL, SD, C:S).
- Loadout selection under a tradeoff (payload vs range, cost vs power) before a mission (F/A-18, CS16).
- Transit as part of the game: things happen on the way, not just at the destination (F/A-18, SD, EV).
- Debrief/score card after each mission feeding campaign progression (F/A-18, SimCopter).
- Dynamic jobs that appear from world state; choose which to take, the others expire (SimCopter, EV mission computer).
- Ally recruitment that changes what you can do in the next encounter (TMOS, EV escorts).
- Two world states (past/future, day/night, peace/war) where acting in one alters the other (TMOS).
- Player-set goals in a sandbox: the systems generate wants, the game does not assign them (RPGWO, EV).
- Persistent world that remembers what the player built or left behind (RPGWO, C:S).
- Capturing or converting defeated enemies into assets (EV ship capture, WC2 raise dead).
- Escalating difficulty by moving to a bigger city, deeper system, or harder theater (SimCopter, C:S, F/A-18).
- Bosses or capstone challenges that verify mastery of the systems, then an opt-in ending (SD, TGL).
- Clutch comebacks: a losing position that still has one realistic win path (CS16 defuse, SC base trade).

## Tones and settings that fit

- Space: trade lanes, pirates, factions, carrier ops. Fiction without heavy lore.
- Military and tactical: sorties, squads, round-based ops, chokepoints, loadouts.
- Fantasy of the Warcraft kind: orcs, mages, gold mines, keeps. Also Arabian Nights, desert kingdoms, time portals.
- Survival and frontier: islands, rafts, scarce water, crafting from scraps, isolation as a tool.
- Civic and management: mayor, dispatcher, logistics, budgets, growth curves.
- Sci-fi action: alien planetoid, transforming hero, retro shmup energy.
- Persistent sandbox worlds: a server-like place where the player's mark stays.
- Sensibility is 90s/retro PC and NES: shareware and LAN-party era, functional briefing screens, numbers on a HUD, no voiced cinematics. Text-forward presentation is a feature, not a limitation.
- Tone is earnest and systemic, lightly humorous at most (WC2 unit quips), never grimdark or melodramatic.

## Pacing and feel

- Short decision cycles. Each turn is a buy phase, a sortie, a round, a day on the island, a quarter in the budget. Every turn ends with a visible result.
- Visible numbers. Money, supply, reputation, population, fuel, thirst. The player wants to see the state and do arithmetic on it.
- Systems that interact. Economy feeds military feeds map control feeds economy. One spend should ripple into two other systems.
- Emergent stories over scripted plot. The memorable moments are a clutch defuse, a pirate ambush on a cargo run, a traffic jam that bankrupts a district. The GM should narrate consequences of choices, not deliver a plot.
- Mastery over narrative. The player wants to get better at the system: learn the counters, find the trade route, time the expansion. Repeat play with improved results is the point.
- Macro arcs made of micro loops. Opening (bootstrap), midgame (contest and expand), lategame (scarcity and decisive action), then a clean ending or an opt-in escape.
- Tension and release. A calm planning phase alternating with a sharp risky phase; neither should overstay.
- Freedom with gravity. Multiple valid paths (trader, fighter, builder) but scarcity and depletion keep the player moving.
- Fair but unforgiving. Dying or losing a round is cheap to recover from in turns but should cost something real (money, cargo, position).

## Avoid

- No heavy narrative. No long lore dumps, no prologue chapters, no chosen-one plot. A one-paragraph premise is enough.
- No dialogue trees as the core. NPC talk should be functional: a job board, a rumor, a price, a hint.
- No pure puzzle games. Logic puzzles, riddles, and adventure-game inventory puzzles are not the draw.
- No twitch or reflex play. Aim, dodging, and APM cannot exist in chat; translate them into positioning, loadout, timing, and odds instead.
- No invisible state. Do not hide the numbers the player is managing; hidden enemy info (fog of war) is fine, hidden own-state is not.
- No railroaded turn-by-turn scripting. The player should be able to ignore the "intended" path and still have a valid game.
- No cosmetic choices without mechanical consequence. Every option offered should change a number or a future option.
- No endless grind without a horizon. Give a milestone ladder or an opt-in ending so progress is always visible.
