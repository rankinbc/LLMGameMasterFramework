# BLACK LANTERNS - playtest summary (game 05)

## Game

**Title:** Black Lanterns

**Premise:** The Navy has withdrawn from the Tern Islands, leaving you as keeper of the last Admiralty light on Beacon Rock with one boat and a store of lamp oil that the Rock burns through whether you move or not. Eight islets ring the Rock, each with a wreck below it that can be dived deck by deck for silver, and each with a beacon cairn that you or the pirate captain Sable Morrow can light. Once you sail away from an islet its channel is marked FOUL and you can never go back, so every islet is dived, lit, and left in one pass. Silver buys oil and gear at Beacon Rock; oil buys sails and beacons. Whoever holds more lit beacons at the end of turn 16 owns the archipelago.

**Rolls:** SET=11 ROLE=32 LOOP=21 CLOCK=1 TWIST=14 WIN=6 MAP=5 TONE=6

- SET 11 Pirate archipelago became the Tern Islands, eight islets in two rings around Beacon Rock.
- ROLE 32 Lighthouse keeper with a boat became the keeper of the Admiralty light, sailing a single boat.
- LOOP 21 Dig deeper became the three-deck wreck dive: 2+/3+/4+ on d6 for rising silver, with collapse on a fail.
- CLOCK 1 Fuel runs out became oil: 1 per turn for the Rock, 1 per sail, 2 per beacon; oil 0 is the loss.
- TWIST 14 Each location once became the FOUL channel rule; it also means home is reachable only through an inner islet you have not yet left.
- WIN 6 Most nodes became the beacon tally versus Morrow's black lanterns, ties to her.
- MAP 5 Two layers became surface islets (where beacons and the rival live) and the wreck decks below (where silver lives).
- TONE 6 Military procedural became keeper's-log voice, ship names, bearings, FOUL channel markings.
- Signature details: the named rival Sable Morrow and her sloop Vixen; the Fresnel lens hidden on deck 3 of the lightship Tern (never reached); the Resolute's powder magazine making deck 2 at Hangman's Key cost 3 oil on a fail.

**Rulesheet (verbatim):**

1. One order per turn: SAIL to a linked islet (1 oil), DIVE one deck deeper at this islet's wreck, LIGHT this islet's beacon (2 oil), or HOME to Beacon Rock (1 oil) to buy oil and gear.
2. Beacon Rock burns 1 oil every turn regardless. Oil 0 means the great light dies and you lose.
3. Wrecks have 3 decks. Deck 1: d6 need 2+, pays 2 silver. Deck 2: need 3+, 4 silver. Deck 3: need 4+, 7 silver. Outer-ring wrecks pay +2 per deck. A failed roll collapses the wreck: dive over, lose 2 oil.
4. When you sail away from an islet its channel is marked FOUL. You can never return there or pass through it, so dive and light before you leave.
5. Captain Morrow's sloop Vixen lights black lanterns on free islets. You may LIGHT an islet she holds: d6 need 4+ (2 oil; on a fail she keeps it and you lose 1 more oil).
6. At Beacon Rock: oil costs about 2 silver each (price drifts). Diving Bell 6 silver (+1 on every dive roll). Chart 3 silver (reveals Morrow's next two targets).
7. Links: Beacon Rock touches the four inner islets. Each inner islet touches its two inner neighbours and one outer islet. Each outer islet touches its two outer neighbours.

Goal: Hold more lit beacons than Morrow when turn 16 ends (ties go to her). Lose if: Oil hits 0. Turns: 16.

## Playthrough

The player opened by doing the oil arithmetic aloud (16 of 24 casks go to the Rock's own burn before any move) and sailed east toward the pirate to deny her the nearest islets. Morrow lit the Anvil the same evening. At Hangman's Key the first dive paid 2 silver and revealed a powder magazine on deck 2; the player pushed deck 2 at two-in-three odds, rolled a 2, and lost 3 oil to the explosion while Morrow lit Saltmarsh Cay. The player lit Hangman's Key for a first beacon, then committed to an outer-ring loop (Anvil, Widow Reef, Gull Spit, home) to reach the richer 4/6/9 wrecks. The Anvil's deck 1 collapsed on a roll of 1, costing 2 more oil, and Morrow took Coral Bar, leaving every eastern door home either FOUL or hers. At Widow Reef the player asked for status, declined a coin-flip contest for the Anvil, and dived deck 1 of the lightship Tern: another 1, another 2 oil. With 4 oil, 2 silver and home two sails off, the player computed that no route home paid before the Rock went dark, lit Widow Reef as a last beacon (which diverted Morrow to Blackwater Hole), and sailed for Gull Spit as the great light died. Result: loss on turn 10 of 16. Final numbers: Oil 0, Silver 2, Beacons 2 vs Vixen 4.

## Rules fit

The game stayed inside the caps: 7 rule lines, 3 tracked numbers (oil, silver, beacon tally), one decision per turn, 3 to 4 options every turn, status line in the same format every turn, every turn under 180 words, all dice shown. The rival's beacon count rides inside the tally as "2 vs Vixen 4", which is one number by the skill's reading but is really two values the player watches.

Things the GM had to clarify or rule mid-game:
- The rulesheet never states that Beacon Rock is only reachable through an inner islet you have not left. It follows from rules 4 and 7 but it is the single most important consequence in the game, and it had to be stated as a one-line note on turn 1.
- Diving at an islet the rival holds is not mentioned in the rules; the GM allowed it.
- Whether buying oil on a HOME order happens before or after the Rock's burn was never tested but would have mattered.
- "Stand your watch here" was offered as a no-op order on the last turn; the rulesheet has no wait order.

## Decisions

Ten decisions were made (the status call did not consume a turn).

- Genuinely hard: 6 (turn 1 direction, turn 2 dive-first versus light-first, turn 3 push deck 2 against the magazine, turn 5 outer loop versus inner safety, turn 7 contest versus move on, turn 9 light here versus run for home).
- Obvious or forced: 4 (turn 4 light the islet you just spent three turns on, turns 6 and 8 take the five-in-six deck 1 dive, turn 10 last cask).

## Emergence

1. Turn 1: Morrow's first black lantern lit on the Anvil, exactly the islet the player was heading for next, turning a plan into a contest before any dive.
2. Turn 3: the hidden magazine quirk turned a normal 2-oil failure into a 3-oil explosion and set the whole oil crisis in motion.
3. Turn 6: the Anvil collapse plus Morrow's claim on Coral Bar meant both eastern doors to Beacon Rock were FOUL or hers in the same turn, so the twist and the rival combined to box the player onto the outer ring.
4. Turn 9: the player's late beacon on Widow Reef sat in Morrow's hidden target order, so she skipped it and lit Blackwater Hole instead; the player's claim visibly redirected the rival.
5. Turns 8 to 9: the FOUL rule and the oil clock together produced a "no line home" state the player could prove by arithmetic, which is emergent but not fun.

Count: 5 (4 events plus 1 structural).

## Problems

- The oil economy does not close. The Rock's 16-turn burn alone eats two-thirds of the starting 24 oil. A dive turn earns about 3 silver on average, which is about 1.5 oil at price 2, minus the 1 oil burn: net about +0.5 oil per dive turn, and sail and light turns are pure loss. The player saw this on turn 1 and was right.
- Dive failure costing oil stacks a second penalty on top of the clock. Three fails out of four dives (about a 1 percent streak, but still) ended the game by turn 10 with no recovery lever. The taste profile asks for loss-streak compensation; this game has none.
- The FOUL twist is the best idea in the game but, combined with the oil clock, it is a trap: once the player went to the outer ring, home was three sails away and every sail cost two oil (passage plus burn). This needed to be visible on the rulesheet.
- Morrow's claim rate (1 in 3 per turn, 4 claims in 9 turns) outpaces anything the player can do, since a player beacon costs at least two turns and funding. Even with average dice the player would struggle to reach 5 of 8.
- Half the design never came online: the shop, the price drift, the Diving Bell, the Chart, the Fresnel lens, the raid-on-a-6, Coral Bar's shallow wreck. Silver did nothing all game.
- Suggested fixes for a rerun: start at 30 oil or drop the ambient burn on dive turns; failed dives lose the deck but not oil (or 1 oil); deck 1 auto-succeeds; Morrow claims on a 1 only; allow passing through a FOUL channel at 2 oil.

## Fun rating

5 out of 10. The skeleton is exactly the kind of thing I like: a push-your-luck dive, a visible tally race against a named rival, a map that closes behind me, and I was doing arithmetic every single turn. But I could tell on turn 1 that the oil math did not close, and three bad d6s turned a tight game into a slow drowning with nothing I could do about it, which is losing to the numbers rather than to my choices.

## Would replay

Yes, but only with the oil numbers fixed. The loop-plan-then-close-the-door structure and the deck-by-deck push are a puzzle I want another shot at, and I never got to touch the shop or the lens. As written, no; the game is decided by the burn rate before the first dive.
